# NYC Zoning Resolution - Article II (Residence District Regulations)
# DEMO CORPUS: plain-language paraphrase for development. Replace with official text from zr.planning.nyc.gov.

New York City has ten basic residence districts, numbered R1 through R10.
The numbers generally indicate permitted density: R1 and R2 districts are low-density, detached single-family areas, while R10 districts permit the highest residential density in the city.

R1 and R2 districts permit only single-family detached residences, along with certain community facilities.
R3 through R5 districts are lower-density districts that permit a mix of detached, semi-detached, and attached houses and small apartment buildings, depending on the specific district.

Medium- and high-density residence districts, R6 through R10, are typically mapped in the more densely built parts of the city.
In these districts, buildings may be developed under the Quality Housing regulations, which produce buildings with street walls set at or near the street line and limited height.

A letter suffix, such as A, B, D, or X, designates a contextual district.
Contextual districts, such as R6A or R7A, set mandatory base heights and maximum building heights so that new buildings match the scale and form of the existing neighborhood.

In R10 districts, the basic maximum residential floor area ratio is 10.0.
Floor area bonuses, where available, can allow additional floor area above the basic maximum in certain high-density districts.

Required yards apply in residence districts.
A front yard is required in lower-density districts, and rear yards are generally required in all residence districts to provide light and air between buildings.

Accessory off-street parking requirements for residences vary by district and location.
The City of Yes for Housing Opportunity amendments adopted in 2024 eliminated or reduced parking requirements for new housing in many parts of the city.
